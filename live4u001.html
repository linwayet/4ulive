In contrast to the packed itineraries of traditional tourism, a growing movement known as "slow travel" encourages visitors to spend more time in fewer destinations. Rather than rushing from one famous landmark to the next, slow travelers prioritize deep immersion in local culture — eating where residents eat, learning a few phrases of the local language, and exploring off-the-beaten-path neighborhoods.

Proponents argue that slow travel creates more meaningful experiences for visitors while also reducing the environmental impact of excessive transportation. Critics, however, point out that the luxury of spending weeks in a single location is not available to everyone, making slow travel an option accessible largely to the privileged. Despite this ongoing debate, the movement continues to grow, particularly among younger generations who prioritize authentic experience over efficient sightseeing.
